Four things that make distributed agile actually work.

01

Overlap hours are protected, not optional

Every team has a real-time window where every location is online together. That window is protected for standups and decisions, not left to chance.

02

Async is the default, not the fallback

Written updates, recorded walkthroughs, and a single shared backlog mean work doesn't stall just because someone's asleep.

03

One backlog, not four

Every location works from the same prioritized backlog. Nobody is quietly running their own parallel roadmap.

04

One delivery manager owns the sprint

Distributed teams still need a single accountable owner for the sprint outcome, not a rotating cast of leads.

Distributed isn't the trade-off. It's the advantage.

A team that only works in one time zone stops when the day ends. Ours keeps moving, with one person still accountable for where it lands.

The practices behind the rhythm.

Shared backlog, always visible

Every location works from the same prioritized tracker, updated in real time, so priority is never a guessing game across time zones.

Recorded, not repeated

Standups and planning sessions are recorded and summarized in writing, so no one has to choose between sleep and being informed.

Rotating overlap windows

When a project needs more real-time collaboration, the overlap window shifts to fit the work, not the other way around.

One roadmap, reviewed weekly

The delivery manager reviews the roadmap with every location weekly, so priorities never drift apart between sites.
